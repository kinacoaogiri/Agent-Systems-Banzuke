# Rules

## 1. Scope

Agent Systems Banzuke ranks publicly assessable **agent systems engineering systems**, not base-model intelligence in isolation.

## 2. Fact First

Assessment must distinguish:

- **System Fact** — what was actually implemented, demonstrated, operated, or otherwise evidenced by the cutoff.
- **Banzuke Fact** — what the assessment recognized and what rank was actually published.

Both are historical facts.

Once a Banzuke result is published, it is not silently rewritten because stronger or previously missed evidence is later found. Later findings are appended as **Retrospective Findings** and may affect subsequent Basho.

Pure clerical factual errors (for example a wrong project name, commit, date, or link) may be corrected, with the correction recorded explicitly.

## 3. Divisions

### Open Division
All eligible systems.

### Team Division
Systems with two or more human development/research principals responsible for the system's principal architecture or implementation decisions.

### Individual Division
Systems whose principal architecture and implementation decisions are led by one human. AI agents do not count as additional humans.

The same rubric applies to all divisions. There is no solo bonus, organization-size penalty, popularity bonus, or vendor bonus.

## 4. Banzuke ranks

Ranks are:

1. Yokozuna
2. Ozeki
3. Sekiwake
4. Komusubi
5. Maegashira

Yokozuna through Komusubi are absolute qualification classes. A rank may therefore be vacant.

Maegashira requires the minimum qualification defined in the rubric and is limited to **Maegashira 1 through Maegashira 5 on each side**: at most ten systems per division.

Within the same rank, East precedes West.

## 5. Torikumi

When systems have the same rank and the same recognized total score, their relative placement is decided by a public **Torikumi**.

Torikumi does not change capability scores or rank qualifications. A lower-scoring system cannot defeat a higher-scoring system through popularity voting.

The official Torikumi mechanism should use a discrete public poll where available. Comments and reactions may accompany the bout, but do not alter the official result unless explicitly designated in the published bout rules.

If a Torikumi itself ends tied, the result must be recorded as tied until an explicit tie procedure is adopted; assessors must not invent an ordering.

## 6. Monoii

A **Monoii** is an evidence-backed challenge to the immediately preceding published Basho result.

- Filing period: the **1st through 14th** following publication.
- Only the immediately preceding result may be challenged.
- Concrete evidence is mandatory.
- Mere disagreement, reputation, popularity, or unsupported claims are insufficient.
- Initial adjudication authority is the project maintainers under the process in `GOVERNANCE.md`.

A sustained Monoii produces a recorded finding and may affect the next Basho. It does not erase the already published historical Banzuke.

## 7. Basho calendar

Normal monthly cycle:

- **1st:** previous Basho result published; Monoii opens.
- **1st–14th:** Monoii period and evidence collection.
- **14th:** evidence cutoff for the coming Basho.
- **15th–last day:** Torikumi period.
- **1st of following month:** Banzuke result published.

The system is assessed as it exists at the cutoff. Earlier public evidence remains usable when it is still relevant to that state; the monthly evidence period is not a rule that erases older valid evidence.

### September 2026 bootstrap Basho

- Torikumi: **2026-09-15 through 2026-09-30**
- First Banzuke publication: **2026-10-01**

## 8. Historical integrity

Published Basho records must identify their evaluation cutoff and, where practical, evaluated versions or commits.

The governing historical rule is:

> **Append, don't rewrite.**
