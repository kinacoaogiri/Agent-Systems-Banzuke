# Governance

## 1. Assessment authority

The initial assessment panel consists of independent fresh-read reviews by:

- GPT
- Claude
- Copilot
- Human adjudicator

The AI reviewers are reviewers, not votes. The Human is the final authority holder for publication under this initial governance model.

## 2. Fresh-read principle

Each AI reviewer should receive the same applicable rubric and evidence corpus and assess it independently, without using another reviewer's conclusion as authority.

For each of the five domains, a review should report at minimum:

- proposed Capability Level;
- proposed Evidence Level;
- resulting Recognized Level;
- evidence supporting the determination;
- material uncertainty or contrary evidence.

## 3. Disagreement

Reviewer disagreement is not resolved by majority vote.

Example:

`GPT: 4 / Claude: 4 / Copilot: 3`

is not automatically a score of 4. The disagreement is returned to the rubric and evidence to identify the reason for divergence. The Human adjudicator then records a reasoned determination.

## 4. Human adjudication

The Human adjudicator must apply the published rubric and evidence rules rather than personal preference, project popularity, organizational prestige, or model reputation.

Material adjudication decisions should be traceable to public evidence.

## 5. Conflict of interest

Affiliation with, maintenance of, contribution to, or other material interest in an assessed system should be disclosed in its assessment record where applicable.

Such a system is not automatically excluded. It receives the same rubric and public-evidence requirements as every other system.

## 6. Monoii adjudication

During the initial project phase, project maintainers adjudicate evidence-backed Monoii.

If the community grows, additional independent Shimpan/reviewers may be introduced. Any future governance change should be published prospectively and must not silently alter historical Basho records.

## 7. Torikumi

Torikumi is a community tie-break only after the technical assessment has produced the same rank and same recognized total score.

Community voting cannot:

- raise or lower a capability level;
- waive a mandatory rank condition;
- promote a lower score over a higher score;
- manufacture a Yokozuna when the Yokozuna qualification is unmet.

## 8. Publication record

Each published assessment should preserve enough information for an independent reader to understand:

- the system assessed;
- division eligibility;
- evidence cutoff;
- evaluated version or commit where practical;
- five Capability Levels;
- five Evidence Levels;
- five Recognized Levels;
- total score;
- mandatory-rank-condition result;
- final rank and East/West placement;
- Torikumi result if applicable;
- later Retrospective Findings or Corrections without erasing the original result.
